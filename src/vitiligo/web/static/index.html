What the trials reported

Registered JAK inhibitor trials in vitiligo that have posted results, at week 24 — the primary endpoint of all of them, and so the only week at which they can be compared. Each drug is shown beside the comparator arm of its own trial.

How to read this, and what is left out
  • Facial and whole-body scores are different scales. F-VASI measures the face, T-VASI the whole body, and the same drug reaches a far higher figure on the face. They are never comparable with each other.
  • A rate without its comparator is not an effect. Vehicle arms in these trials reached 7–32% depending on the threshold, so the column that matters is the difference. Arms whose trial had no comparator are hidden by default.
  • Patients who left the trial are counted as not responding. Figures counted only among those still attending run higher and are excluded.
  • Where a trial reported both a blinded central reading and the site's own, the central one is shown. It is consistently the lower of the two.
  • Each figure says which patients it counted, and the trials differ. Most report only the patients assessed at that visit, so people who stopped coming are absent from both the numerator and the denominator. Two impute a missing result as a failure. TRuE-V does the first at week 52 and the second at week 24, which is why its two figures are labelled differently and should not be read as one trajectory. Figures measured on different populations are not interchangeable.
  • "Also" lines are other trials of the same drug and dose. Two independent trials reaching the same figure is far stronger than one reaching it. The headline row is the largest arm; the others are listed beside it.
  • The one-year column is a range, not a single figure. TRuE-V reports week 52 among the patients assessed then — 177 of the 222 it had at week 24 — and says nothing about the other 45. Counting them all as failures gives the low end, counting them all as successes the high end, and the answer is somewhere between. The published 48% sits near the top of that range because it quietly leaves them out. The range assumes nothing about them, and its width is the price of the missing data.
  • The one-year column has no comparator. After week 24 these trials put every patient on the drug, so it shows one group improving over time, with the number still being counted. It is not a controlled result.
  • What is missing: adverse events, which belong in the label and the registry record, not a summary table; durability after stopping; and any trial that has not posted results. Dose comparisons within one trial were not powered as such.
