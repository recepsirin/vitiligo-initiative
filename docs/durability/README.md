# Durability: does repigmentation last when JAK treatment is reduced or stopped?

Outcome 3 of [what success means](../success.md). This page maps the evidence and sets out the first tests.

**The question.** For a patient who has responded to a JAK inhibitor:
- how likely is the response to last if treatment is **stopped** or the dose **reduced**;
- how quickly is it lost;
- who keeps it?

## What exists: one randomized withdrawal study

**TRuE-V long-term extension** ([NCT04530344](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04530344?tab=results); results posted 2023-07-19; [extracted](data/nct04530344-results.json)), ruxolitinib 1.5% cream:
- **Who was randomized.** Patients with near-complete facial repigmentation (≥ F-VASI90) at week 52.
- **To what.** 1:1 to continue the cream (55) or switch to vehicle (56), for 52 more weeks.

| Outcome, randomized analysis | Stopped (vehicle) | Continued | Comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to relapse, defined as < F-VASI75 | Median not reached (lower 95% limit 238 days) | Median not reached | Hazard ratio 0.42 (95% CI 0.18–0.99), p = 0.04 |
| Time to losing F-VASI90 | **Median 195 days** (95% CI 113–372) | Median not reached | Hazard ratio 0.32 (0.17–0.61), p < 0.001 |

The registry also posts response by visit for patients still assessed.

| Week 52 → 104, among those assessed | Stopped | Continued |
|---|---|---|
| F-VASI90 | 98% → 70% | 97% → 92% |
| T-VASI50 | 81% → 61% | 72% → 90% |

**What it says.**
- **The face mostly held.** For near-complete responders to a cream, most facial repigmentation lasted a year without treatment.
- **The best results eroded.** Near-complete facial response, and body response, wore off within months. Continuing treatment protected both.

**Caveats.**
- **The by-visit table flatters durability.** It counts only patients still assessed; those who relapsed and took rescue treatment drop out (about 23 of the 57 who stopped were still assessed at week 104). The randomized time-to-event rows are the valid comparison.
- **Highly selected patients.** These were near-complete responders, the people most likely to keep their response.
- **Topical only.** We do not know whether stopping an oral drug behaves the same.
- **Proportions not public.** Kaplan–Meier proportions are in two 2026 papers (Br J Dermatol; J Am Acad Dermatol post hoc analysis of treatment duration), neither open access.

**No oral JAK inhibitor has posted randomized withdrawal data.** Older maintenance trials of other treatments posted no results: tacrolimus (NCT01841008, terminated), NB-UVB and excimer laser (NCT03384342, NCT03384459, status unknown).

## Across treatments: durability is poor, and maintenance may help

From published abstracts. These numbers are not curated rows: the papers are not open access.

| Study | Design | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| NB-UVB, "does the repigmentation last?" (J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol 2007, PMID 17658996) | 11 responders (> 75% repigmentation) followed for 2 years after stopping | 6 relapsed, 4 of them within 6 months; 5 of 31 treated (16%) had stable > 75% repigmentation at 2 years |
| NB-UVB, long-term follow-up (J Am Acad Dermatol 2007, PMID 17224369) | 70 patients, uncontrolled | Repigmentation stable in 14.3% of patients 4 years after stopping |
| Tacrolimus 0.1% twice weekly as maintenance (J Invest Dermatol 2015, PMID 25521460) | Randomized, double-blind vs placebo after successful repigmentation; 35 patients, 72 patches | Depigmentation in 48.4% of placebo vs 26.8% of tacrolimus lesions (intention to treat, p = 0.059; not significant after adjusting for patients' multiple lesions); per protocol, 40% vs 9.7% |

- **Relapse after stopping is the rule across treatments.** The tacrolimus paper puts it at about 40% within the first year after successful repigmentation.
- **The one randomized maintenance trial is small** and its intention-to-treat result is not significant.

## Strategies aimed at durability (hypotheses, untested)

1. **Step down to a light maintenance regimen** instead of stopping: twice-weekly topical tacrolimus, or less frequent ruxolitinib cream. Tacrolimus maintenance has one small trial (above). No trial has tested maintenance after JAK-induced repigmentation.
2. **Treat longer before stopping.** A 2026 post hoc analysis of TRuE-V studies "impact of treatment duration on response durability" (J Am Acad Dermatol, PMID 41985560). Its results are not public in open access.
3. **Target the memory directly.** Lesional T cells in vitiligo have a resident-memory phenotype, and blocking IL-15 signalling (anti-CD122) reversed established vitiligo in mice and depleted those cells from lesions (Richmond et al., Sci Transl Med 2018, PMID 30021889).
   - An anti-IL-15 antibody, IBI3013, is in a phase 1 trial that includes patients with active vitiligo ([NCT07554222](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07554222); recruiting; primary completion estimated 2027-12).
   - This is the only strategy aimed at problem 3 itself rather than at suppressing the attack.

## What is coming: two randomized tests of an oral JAK inhibitor

Designs from the registry and the published protocol description ([PMC13280265](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13280265)).

| | **Stopping:** Tranquillo LTE ([NCT06163326](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06163326)) | **Tapering:** Tranquillo 2, Part Ib ([NCT06072183](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06072183)) |
|---|---|---|
| Who is randomized | Responders to ritlecitinib 50 mg at week 52 of Tranquillo (≥ F-VASI75 and/or ≥ T-VASI50) | Responders to ritlecitinib 100 mg at week 52 (same definition) |
| Comparison (1:1, 52 weeks) | Continue 50 mg vs **switch to placebo** | Continue 100 mg vs **step down to 50 mg** |
| Loss of response | < F-VASI75 **and** < T-VASI50 at the same visit | Responses at weeks 56–104 |
| Status | Enrolled 394; primary completion 2027-03 (estimated) | Part Ia primary completion 2027-07 (estimated); Part Ib follows |
| Expected size | Small: Tranquillo's 50 mg arm (401) had 12.5% F-VASI75 and 9% T-VASI50 responders, so about 50–90 responders in total, roughly 25–45 per arm | Larger: Tranquillo 2's 100 mg arm (622) had 21.9% F-VASI75 and 13% T-VASI50 |

Neither has results, so forecasts sealed now are prospective. Povorcitinib's phase 3 trials continue to week 104, but without randomized withdrawal; they will show only whether response keeps building on continued treatment.

## The first tests

**1. Stopping: sealed 2026-10-23** ([pre-registration](../durability-forecast-preregistration.md), [record](../../forecasts/durability/tranquillo-lte-withdrawal.json)). In Tranquillo LTE's randomized withdrawal, the forecast for loss of response within 52 weeks is:

| | Median | 80% range |
|---|---|---|
| Still responding after stopping | 42% | 14–76% |
| Still responding on continued 50 mg | 75% | 42–92% |
| Hazard ratio, continue vs stop | 0.35 | 0.17–0.69 |

- **What the forecast is.** It is fixed from TRuE-V alone: two analogs weighted 2:1, with explicit uncertainty for transfer from topical to oral.
- **It gives a 61% chance that most responders lose their response within a year of stopping.**
- **How it will be judged.** The topical evidence counts as transferring if the forecast's log score for the posted hazard ratio beats an uninformed forecast's.

**What the test can tell us** (post hoc: computed after sealing, though it was planned before; [`informativeness.json`](data/informativeness.json), `scripts/durability/informativeness.py`). The trial is simulated at 25–45 per arm:

| If the truth is | Forecast beats the benchmark | Hazard ratio estimable |
|---|---|---|
| As forecast | 83–85% | 95–98% |
| Continuing does nothing (HR 1) | 9–19% | 100% |
| Responses mostly last after stopping (90%, HR 0.5) | 65–68% | 69–90% |

- **The primary test checks how much continuing helps.** It would refute the forecast if continuing made little difference.
- **It cannot tell how many people lose response after stopping.** A world where most responses last still scores the forecast as a win most of the time.
- **So whether "most lose response within a year" is right depends on the secondary score**, which needs Kaplan–Meier retention at week 52 to be reported.
- **If responses mostly last, events are few** and the hazard ratio may not be estimable.

**2. Tapering: waits for Tranquillo 2's Part Ia results.** How much 50 mg keeps compared with 100 mg is best informed by Part Ia's 50 vs 100 mg comparison. That will be public before Part Ib's results, so the forecast is sealed then, still prospectively, rather than guessed now.

## A trial that would answer it: stop or step down

A concept for an investigator-initiated trial: responders to ruxolitinib cream are randomized to twice-weekly tacrolimus maintenance or to stopping, and the trial runs until 66 losses of response have occurred. That is about 64–185 patients per arm, depending on how often responses are lost after stopping: [`maintenance-trial-concept.md`](maintenance-trial-concept.md). It needs a clinical lead.

## Who keeps it: patient-level data

"Who keeps their response" needs patient-level data.
- **Topical (ruxolitinib cream).** Incyte's published policy appears to allow a request for TRuE-V's data now. A request is drafted: [`data-request-draft.md`](data-request-draft.md). It needs a named team with a biostatistician before it can be sent.
- **Oral (ritlecitinib).** Pfizer shares data only for approved indications, 18 months after primary completion, so the Tranquillo data are not available until ritlecitinib is approved for vitiligo.

## What the forecast had to handle

- **No oral data.** The only randomized withdrawal data are for a cream, so the forecast must carry an explicit, wide uncertainty about transfer from topical to oral.
- **Different definitions.** TRuE-V randomized near-complete facial responders and counted relapse below F-VASI75. Tranquillo LTE randomizes anyone above F-VASI75 or T-VASI50, and counts loss as falling below both.
- **Only summaries posted.** From the registry we have hazard ratios and medians, not event counts. The two 2026 papers report more, and need full-text access.
- **Small arms.** Uncertainty will be wide; see what the test can tell us, above.
