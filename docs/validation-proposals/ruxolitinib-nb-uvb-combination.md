## Hypothesis

**Adding or sequencing narrowband UVB (NB-UVB) with topical ruxolitinib cream improves repigmentation durability and speed versus ruxolitinib monotherapy in stable non-segmental vitiligo (NSV)**, particularly in body regions with poor baseline response.

## Why now

- Topical ruxolitinib is **approved** for vitiligo in the US; real-world and trial literature shows regional heterogeneity in repigmentation.
- Multiple registered trials explore ruxolitinib combinations (e.g. NCT07368673: ruxolitinib vs ruxolitinib + tacrolimus + corticosteroids).
- Literature in the engine corpus strongly retrieves ruxolitinib + NB-UVB combination papers (semantic scores ~0.90).

## Proposed study (investigator-initiated, outline)

| Element | Proposal |
|---------|----------|
| Design | Randomized, open-label or pragmatic comparative (ruxolitinib alone vs ruxolitinib → NB-UVB add-on at week 8–12 non-responders) |
| Population | Stable NSV, ≥18y, F-VASI baseline documented, exclude active rapid spread |
| Primary endpoint | Change in F-VASI / T-VASI at 24 weeks |
| Secondary | Time to first repigmentation, regional response, patient-reported outcomes |
| N (pilot) | 40–60 (feasibility); power for confirmatory design after pilot |
| Duration | 6-month treatment + 3-month follow-up |
